Lesson 4

The Hiker in the Fog

This is the heart of SPAM: how it turns a rough guess into a precise answer.

Lost in the fog

Imagine you're on a foggy hillside, looking for the lowest point in the valley. You can't see the valley, but you can feel the slope under your feet. So you step downhill, feel again, and step again. When the ground is flat, you stop. In SPAM, height is the error, and your position is the guess.

SPAM doesn't test every possible position here, because that would take far too long. It starts from a guess and improves it. That's why this step can reach a small fraction of a voxel.

Try both starts! From close, the bumps overlap, so the difference tells SPAM which way to go. From far, they don't overlap at all. The push is zero, so SPAM takes a tiny step and thinks it's finished. It isn't lying. It just can't see anything from there.

The two clues: A and M

Each step needs just two ingredients, built from the image's gradient, which tells you how fast brightness changes at each voxel, so it acts like the slope of the bone pattern.

ClueHow it's madeIn plain words
ASum of gradient × differenceWhich way to push, and how hard.
MSum of gradient × gradientHow much texture the window has, so how far a push moves it.
M · ΔΦ = A   so   ΔΦ = M⁻¹ · A

With one unknown, that's a simple division, as in the lab above. Phi has 12 unknowns, so A is a list of 12 numbers and M is a 12 × 12 table, but the idea is identical.

The loop is short. Compute M and A, then solve deltaPhi = dot(inv(M), A). Then update the guess with Phi = dot(Phi, eye(4) + deltaPhi), redraw the image with the new Phi, and work out the error. A smart shortcut: the gradient is computed once on the still image, so M is built only on the first iteration and reused. Only A is recalculated each time, which saves a lot of time. The -ug option turns this shortcut off.

See the far start? The hiker found a small dip on the hillside and stopped there. The ground is flat, so SPAM says "converged", but it's the wrong place. The error stays high, and that's our clue. A real answer needs a good starting guess, and that's what the next lessons are about.

When does SPAM stop?

Every window ends with a returnStatus, like a report card.

StatusWhat happened
2The step became tiny, below deltaPhiMin = 0.001. Converged.
1It hit the iteration limit, which is 50 in spam-ldic.
−1The error got much worse, so it's probably diverging.
−2M couldn't be inverted, usually because there's too little texture.
−3The window's volume changed impossibly, by more than 3 times or below 0.2 times.
−4Phi couldn't be inverted, usually from divergence.
−5Skipped, for example masked out or too dark.
−7Skipped because the starting Phi was empty.
Big warning: status 2 means "I stopped moving", not "I'm right". Our far start just proved that. So we always check the result another way too.

Remember this

  • SPAM improves a guess in steps, using the difference and the gradient.
  • Each step solves M · ΔΦ = A.
  • It only sees nearby, so the starting guess really matters.
  • Status 2 means it stopped, not that it's definitely right.
