Lesson 1

The Sticker Game

DVC answers one question: where did this little piece of bone go?

Imagine a block of jelly

Take a block of jelly with lots of tiny sprinkles inside. Take a 3D photo. Then squeeze the jelly and take another. The sprinkles have moved, but their pattern is still recognisable. If you can find the same little group of sprinkles in both photos, you know how far that part of the jelly moved.

Bone works the same way. A vertebra has a sponge-like inside, and those struts and spaces make a unique pattern. We don't add sprinkles, because the bone already has its own.

You just did DVC for one window! You compared patterns and found the best match. The arrow from the old position to the new one is called the displacement. SPAM does this for thousands of windows, in 3D, and more precisely than a whole voxel.

The words you need

Reference imageThe unloaded scan. It stays still. SPAM calls it im1.
Deformed imageThe loaded scan. SPAM calls it im2.
NodeA chosen point in the bone where we measure movement.
WindowThe small box of voxels around a node. SPAM calls it an imagette.
DisplacementHow far that node moved, in z, y and x.
A common setup uses a half-window of 25 voxels, so each window is 51 × 51 × 51 voxels, with nodes 50 voxels apart. With 39 µm voxels, that window is about 2 mm wide, and a vertebra gets a few thousand nodes.

Remember this

  • DVC follows a pattern from one scan to the other.
  • It works on small windows, not the whole bone at once.
  • Each window gives one movement: its displacement.
