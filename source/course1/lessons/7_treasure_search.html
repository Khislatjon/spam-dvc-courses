Lesson 7

The Treasure Search

When the hint is too far from the truth, stop walking and search the whole field.

Hiker or treasure hunter?

The hiker from Lesson 4 is quick and very precise, but only sees nearby. A treasure hunter is different. They divide the field into squares and dig in every single one. It's slow and only accurate to one square, but they can't miss a treasure that's in the field. The best plan is to use both: the hunter finds the right spot, then the hiker gets the exact position.

That's pixel search. For each window, it tests every whole-voxel position in a range, scores each one with correlation, and keeps the best.

The trap: false matches

If you search a huge field, you might find something that looks like treasure but isn't. Bone is repetitive, a bit like a field full of similar rocks. The bigger the search, the more lookalikes there are.

A warning sign is displacements that suddenly become far larger than their neighbours. Using a range built for a badly collapsed sample on a sample that barely moved can make results worse, not better.

Search rangeWhat goes wrong
Too smallThe true match is outside the range, so it can't be found.
Too bigSlower, with more false matches.
About rightJust bigger than the movement you expect beyond registration.

Why searches often run on half-size scans

The work is the number of positions multiplied by the voxels in a window. It grows very quickly.

Search of ±40, ±25, ±10 vx with a 51 vx windowPositionsVoxels per windowComparisons per node
Full size86,751132,651about 11.5 billion
Half size12,17715,625about 190 million

Half size needs about 60 times fewer comparisons. The result is only a rough guess, but that's fine, because local DVC refines it at full size afterwards.

The local search is pixelSearchLocal. It calls getImagettes to cut a window and a bigger search area, then the compiled pixelSearchCPP computes correlation at every position. Results are whole voxels, with no F of their own: it searches shifts only. That's another reason it must be followed by spam-ldic.

Remember this

  • Pixel search is slow and rough, but it can't get stuck.
  • Local DVC is quick and precise, but needs a good start.
  • Use pixel search when movement is large or changes sharply, as near a fracture or lesion.
  • Keep the range just big enough, to avoid false matches.
