Lesson 5

The Blurry Glasses

How does SPAM get a good first guess when the bone may have moved a long way?

Squint at the picture

Vox saysFind a friend across a crowded room. You don't start by reading their shirt buttons! First you look for a tall shape in a red coat. Then you walk closer and check their face. SPAM does the same. It starts with a blurry scan to get the big picture right, then uses sharper scans for the details.

Blurring here is called binning. Bin 2 means every 2 × 2 × 2 block of voxels is averaged into one big voxel. The scan gets 8 times smaller, so it's faster too.

Blurring removes the small bumps in the landscape, leaving a smooth valley that leads toward the answer. In a sharp image, the small dips can trap the hiker.

Vox saysEach rung passes its answer to the next one. When we go from bin 8 to bin 4, one big voxel becomes two smaller ones, so the displacement must be doubled. Then the sharper rung only has to fix a small mistake, which is easy for the hiker.

This is registration

Registration finds one Phi for the whole scan. It gets the big movement right: the slide, the turn and the overall squash. That answer becomes the starting guess for every window.

Real SPAM · spam/DIC/registration.py, registerMultiscale()
The function starts at binStart, runs register, passes Phi down, and stops at binStop. For example, spam-reg -bb 4 -be 2 begins at bin 4 and ends at bin 2. spam.DIC.binning averages neighbouring voxels, and PhiInitBinRatio rescales the translation between levels. Only t is rescaled, because F has no units.
Vox saysOne Phi works well when a sample moves fairly evenly. If part of it collapses or fractures, one Phi can't describe that, and registration may not fully settle. That kind of sample needs more help, which is Lesson 7.

Remember this

  • Binning means averaging voxels. It's blurrier, smaller and faster.
  • Blur smooths the error landscape, so the hiker can travel further.
  • Registration goes from coarse to fine, and gives one Phi for the whole scan.
  • That Phi is the first guess for all the windows.
